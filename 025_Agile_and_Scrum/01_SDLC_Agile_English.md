# Software Development Life Cycle (Agile & Scrum)

## What is it?
The Software Development Life Cycle (SDLC) is the process used by the software industry to design, develop, and test high-quality software. Historically, companies used the **Waterfall** model (a rigid, step-by-step process), but modern teams almost exclusively use **Agile**.

## Agile Methodology
Agile is an iterative approach to software delivery. Instead of delivering everything at once at the very end, Agile teams deliver work in small, consumable increments.
- **Flexibility**: Requirements and plans are expected to change based on customer feedback.
- **Speed**: Working software is delivered frequently (every couple of weeks).

## Scrum Framework
Scrum is the most popular framework used to implement Agile. It provides a specific set of rules, roles, and meetings.

### 1. Scrum Roles
- **Product Owner (PO)**: Represents the customer. They prioritize the backlog (the to-do list of features).
- **Scrum Master**: A facilitator who helps the team follow Scrum rules and removes obstacles blocking their progress.
- **Development Team**: The engineers (backend, frontend, QA) who actually build the product.

### 2. Sprints
A Sprint is a fixed, short timebox (usually 2 weeks) during which a specific set of features is built and made ready for release.

### 3. Scrum Events (Meetings)
- **Sprint Planning**: The team decides what they can commit to building in the upcoming sprint.
- **Daily Stand-up**: A 15-minute daily meeting where everyone says: What did I do yesterday? What will I do today? Are there any blockers?
- **Sprint Review**: At the end of the sprint, the team demonstrates the working software to stakeholders.
- **Sprint Retrospective**: The team discusses what went well, what went wrong, and how to improve the process for the next sprint.

## What Problems Does It Solve?
- Prevents spending months building a product that the customer ultimately doesn't want.
- Increases communication and transparency within the team.
